The building receives you before the room does

Most audits that go wrong do so by starting inside. The kitchen, the bedroom, the desk — these are where clients feel the problem, so it is natural to look there first. But a building’s relationship to its approach, its facing and its first threshold sets the terms for everything behind them.

In practice, a surprising share of what clients describe as an internal problem traces back to the first few metres: how the entrance is reached, what it faces, and what it opens onto. Rearranging furniture rarely resolves what the front door has already decided.

“Furniture is a conversation inside a sentence the front door has already written.”

Date Selection September 2026 7 min read

The best date is often the second-best date

Clients sometimes arrive expecting a single correct day. The almanac rarely offers one. What it offers is a ranked field of candidates, each with trade-offs against the people involved and the constraints already fixed — a lawyer’s availability, a lender’s timeline, a lease that ends when it ends.

A theoretically ideal date that forces a rushed signing is worse than a sound one that lets the work be done properly. Date Selection is a scheduling discipline; it should make a plan more robust, not more fragile.

“A good date does not make a weak decision strong. It removes avoidable friction from a sound one.”

Bazi August 2026 11 min read

Why a chart calculator is not a reading

Generating the Four Pillars is arithmetic. Anyone with the date, time and place of birth can produce the eight characters in seconds. What follows is not arithmetic: judging the strength of the Day Master against its season, identifying what the chart needs, and reading how the luck pillars and the current year move through it.

This is why the calculator on this site stops at the chart. Two identical-looking structures can call for opposite conclusions once season and combination are weighed. Automated summaries collapse that difference, and with it most of the value.

“The eight characters are the evidence. The reading is the judgment.”
